1. Motivation

"Fitness shouldn't depend on your schedule or budget."

FormFit AI's broader product vision is an accessible, flexible, personalized AI fitness coach — not merely a CrossFit whiteboard scanner. That vision responds to several real, common barriers to consistent training:

  • Fixed schedules and limited time: busy professionals and students often cannot attend classes at set times, and frequently have only short windows available — a lunch break, or 20-45 minutes between commitments.
  • Cost of individualized attention: one-on-one personal coaching is expensive, and group classes divide a coach's attention among many participants, limiting individualized feedback and technique supervision.
  • One-size-fits-all programming: standardized workouts do not automatically fit each person's ability, goals, equipment, limitations, or available time.
  • Lack of continuity: athletes training without a dedicated coach often lack continuous progression tracking and personalized follow-up, and nutrition/recovery guidance may require paying for yet another separate service.
  • Sustainability of the habit itself: inconsistent access to training that actually fits a person's life can make long-term exercise habits difficult to sustain.

These are presented as the problems motivating FormFit AI's product direction, not as independently measured statistics — no claim here is backed by a cited study or survey; it is a reasoned product rationale, stated as such.

MVP vs. vision, stated plainly: the working application described in this report today addresses a narrower slice of this vision — CrossFit WOD interpretation, scaling, and basic coaching guidance. The broader vision (flexible scheduling/time-budget optimization, personalized nutrition, continuous progression tracking, video technique correction) is future product direction, not implemented or verified functionality — see §7 for the explicit scope boundary.

"Your schedule. Your level. Your equipment. Your workout."

FormFit AI is built mobile-first: the typical use case is an athlete at the gym, on their phone, photographing a whiteboard WOD, confirming the extraction, and getting a personalized plan — not sitting at a desktop. Responsive mobile behavior across common smartphone viewport sizes is verified in §7/the Technical Audit (see `docs/TECHNICAL_AUDIT.md` "Mobile-First Verification").

2. What Is New

Based on a competitive review of existing CrossFit tracking/coaching products (full detail in docs/COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md), here is what the current working MVP actually does differently — not any single piece in isolation — is new for this space:

  • Whiteboard-photo-to-structured-workout extraction with mandatory human confirmation before the extracted text is ever used (verified real test evidence, §6).
  • A fully integrated single workflow: profile → scan or type → confirm → personalized coaching → persisted history, rather than separate logging/community/coaching tools.
  • A transparent, user-visible agent execution trace for every coaching plan generated (see the real screenshot in §4).
  • Honest uncertainty handling: the system explicitly says "unclear" rather than guessing when handwriting is illegible (real evidence in §6).

This project does not claim to be the first AI-assisted fitness coaching tool in any general sense — see the explicit non-claims in docs/COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md.

To be clear: the broader vision's remaining pain points — flexible time-budget scheduling, personalized nutrition, continuous long-term progression tracking, and video technique correction — are not operational today. No feature in this report should be read as implying they are, unless independently verified and described as such elsewhere in this document (they are not).

3. The Agents

FormFit AI uses a single orchestrating pipeline with five distinct tool calls (not a single opaque prompt, and not a hard-coded sequence disguised as "agentic"):

User Input (typed WOD or whiteboard photo) | v [1] scan_wod_image_tool (LLM, vision) -- only if image uploaded | transcribes photo to text; flags [unclear: ...] rather than guessing v >> USER REVIEWS / EDITS TEXT (mandatory, never auto-applied) << | v [2] parse_wod_tool (LLM) -- extracts structured WOD (format, exercises, scheme) | v [3] validate_wod_tool (local) -- checks exercises against curated knowledge base | v [4] generate_recommendation_tool (LLM) -- warm-up, scaling, guidance, accessories | v [5] validate_recommendation_tool (local) -- Pydantic schema validation | v Saved to SQLite + displayed with real usage/cost + full trace

Architecture reference: backend/app/agent.py. Each step's real input/output/timing is captured into an inspectable trace, shown to the end user (see the Agent Trace panel in Figure 2 below) — not just logged internally and hidden.

Full detail, including redacted real attack traces: docs/SAFETY_EVIDENCE.md. Five genuine real-API tests were run (not mocked):

Ambiguous/illegible imageReturned "[unclear: illegible]" for all lines; zero fabricated contentApplication (prompt design)

Provider vs. application defenses, explicitly distinguished (per instruction, not conflated): the two "rejected by content filter" results are credited to Azure OpenAI's platform, not to code written in this project. Only the verbatim-transcription behavior and the architectural fact that the real API key is never placed in any prompt are genuinely this project's own defenses.

Other controls: upload restrictions (PNG/JPEG/WebP only, 8MB max, verified by automated tests), budget controls (no retry loops, no background calls, 8MB cap limits per-request cost exposure), and exercise-related limitations (recommendations constrained to a curated knowledge base, but this is a content constraint, not a medical safety guarantee — see full disclosure in docs/SAFETY_EVIDENCE.md §5).

This project does not claim complete protection. Five real tests is a sample, not an exhaustive security audit. No automated, repeatable adversarial test suite exists yet — a disclosed gap, not a hidden one.

Video Technique Analysis — Feasibility Experiment and Decision

Before deciding on Video Technique Analysis, a real, isolated feasibility experiment was run (full detail: experiments/video_feasibility/RESULTS.md) — it was never integrated into the production application. What follows separates what was actually observed from what is interpretation.

Observed evidence:

  • Two real Olympic-lifting videos (a squat clean and a snatch, same athlete/session) were evaluated using four extracted frames per video, sent to gpt-6-luna in two real API calls.
  • For the squat clean, the model's identification was likely correct, cross-checked by direct visual inspection of the frames.
  • For the second video, the model could not reliably distinguish the exercise (snatch vs. clean-and-jerk) and explicitly said so rather than guessing.
  • The four static frames allowed only limited observations of visible body/bar positions at each captured instant; a weight plate occluded the lifter's hands/bar/arms in both clips.
  • Four frames did not provide sufficient temporal information to assess complete lifting technique (bar path, tempo, or joint timing through the full movement).
  • The tested configuration produced zero actionable coaching cues in either call.

Interpretation:

  • The safety-constrained system prompt used in this experiment deliberately did not ask the model for technique-fault judgments (per this project's own injury/medical-claim restrictions). The absence of coaching cues therefore does not prove the model is fundamentally incapable of useful feedback — it reflects the conservative prompt design tested, not an exhaustive capability ceiling.
  • Given the combination of unreliable identification on a harder case, real occlusion limitations, and the absence of temporal/motion information from static frames, we decided not to deploy Video Technique Analysis without improved temporal processing (e.g., more frames, pose estimation, or native video support) and expert human validation of any output before it could be shown to an athlete as coaching feedback.

This is presented as a responsible, evidence-based product decision — the experiment's real findings, not assumption, drove the choice to keep this feature as future work rather than ship it.
